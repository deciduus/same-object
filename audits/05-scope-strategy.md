# Scope/strategy audit

## Summary (5 lines max)

- The portfolio is over-weighted in *locating* (≈20 gaps) and *repackaging* (≈20 instruments) and under-weighted in *exploiting*: not one bridge has yet carried a result across to a new prediction, which is exactly what `vault/strategy.md` diagnosed and has not yet acted on.
- The highest-EV thread is **not** C5 alone. C5 is the right *stem*, but the highest EV/cost item in the whole vault is the **C18 Weibull-β axis**, which is one desk session from a checkable, publishable, owner-interest-aligned claim (durability distributions, not means) and currently sits parked.
- The owner's stated interests (ecology, circularity, sustainability, conservation, astrobiology) are almost entirely **unconnected** to the vault's own instruments, despite four of them being near-isomorphic to standing literatures: C1/C6 ↔ industrial-ecology *availability/maintenance*, C18 ↔ *product-lifetime/obsolescence* and *ecological resilience* (recovery-rate vs. return-time), C19 ↔ *subsidy-stress gradient* ecology, C5 ↔ *reserve-selection / patch-abandonment* conservation.
- The UAP thread is correctly diagnosed as testimony-bound and cannot reach a checkable claim in this project's hands; its *instrument* (reservoir + information audit) is transferable and there is a strictly better, data-bearing astrobiology target — **biosignature false-positive/abiotic-baseline auditing** — that runs the same identity→enumerate→exclude→specify template on public JWST/Ariel-class data.
- The buildable register names the right first build (latch fatigue) but its protocol is missing the three things that decide whether the number survives review: a defined failure criterion tied to a distribution rather than a mean, a printed-polymer-vs-cuticle same-object argument, and environmental control (humidity), which is the exact variable the source literature's "dried specimens" flaw turns on.

## New candidate gaps toward owner interests

Same-object test as the project uses: both fields must quantify **the same physical object with commensurable units**, not the same metaphor.

- `reliability availability A = MTBF/(MTBF+MTTR) (C1) ↔ industrial ecology "in-use stock utilisation / idle-capacity fraction" — same object: both are the time-fraction a unit is delivering function, computed from the same up/down partition; the circular-economy literature (Material Flow Analysis; Bocken 2016 slowing/closing loops; the "product utilisation intensity" metric) computes exactly A for artefacts and has never put a tissue on the axis, while C1 has never put a product on it — how to test: citation-intersect "availability"/"MTBF" against "in-use stock"/"utilisation intensity"/"idle capacity" (Journal of Industrial Ecology, Resources Conservation & Recycling), then compute A for a repairable product class and place it in the C1 table beside cortical bone — effort: S`
- `Weibull shape β (C18) ↔ product-lifetime distribution literature (Oguchi & Daigo; Murakami 2010 "lifespan of commodities") — same object: both are the shape parameter of the same failure-time distribution, fitted to the same kind of survival data; the obsolescence literature routinely fits Weibull to appliance lifespans and never talks to catalysis or enzymes — how to test: pull published product-lifespan Weibull fits, place them on the C18 β axis, and ask whether "designed for circularity" products differ in β (wear-out) vs β≈1 (random loss/theft/obsolescence), which is a policy-relevant discriminator nobody has stated — effort: S`
- `Weibull β / hazard shape (C18) ↔ ecological resilience "return time" and "recovery rate" (engineering resilience sensu Pimm; Scheffer critical-slowing-down) — same object: the hazard function of a disturbed system's failure-to-recover, both fields fitting first-passage times to the same mathematical object; resilience ecology reports mean return time only, exactly the mean-not-distribution asymmetry C18 identified in enzymes — how to test: take published disturbance-recovery time series (coral bleaching, grassland drought), fit β, and test whether ecosystems are β≈1 (memoryless) or β>1 (wear-out/degrading resilience) — the latter would be a quantitative statement of "resilience erosion" — effort: M`
- `hormesis biphasic window (C19) ↔ the subsidy–stress gradient in ecology (Odum 1979) and disturbance-intermediate hypotheses — same object: a non-monotonic response of a performance variable to a single dose/intensity variable, with a fitted peak and a fitted zero-crossing; toxicology has the constants, materials science now has two (C19), ecology names the curve and has never parameterised window width — how to test: fit published grazing-intensity or nutrient-loading productivity curves for window width and ceiling, then place them on the C19 axis beside AA7075 and toxicology's 10–20× — effort: S–M`
- `healing Damköhler Ha = k_r/k_d (C6) ↔ repair-vs-replace decision literature in circular economy (remanufacturing rate vs. discard rate) — same object: the ratio of a restoration rate to a degradation rate on the same clock, which is what determines whether a stock is maintainable; the remanufacturing literature computes flows, never the dimensionless ratio — how to test: form Ha for a product-stock model (repair throughput / failure throughput), check it reduces to fleet availability exactly as C6 reduces to A, and report the first Ha values for a repairable product system — effort: S`
- `Kirkwood disposable soma (finite repair budget) ↔ maintenance-budget allocation in infrastructure asset management — same object: allocation of a finite resource between growth/production and repair, with the same optimisation structure; C10 already vindicated Kirkwood as the right frame for the depletion floor, and asset management has the identical trade-off with published budgets — how to test: citation-intersect Kirkwood 1977 against infrastructure asset-management literature (expect 0, matching C13's 5,075-citer result), then map the depletion floor onto a maintenance-budget exhaustion curve — effort: M`
- `Hill numbers (G6) ↔ circular-economy "material circularity indicator" and functional-diversity accounting — same object: an effective-number-of-types computed from the same q-order Rényi family; ecology's N-function framework is the mathematically correct way to count how many functions a material stock serves, which is precisely what multifunctionality metrics for structural batteries and for circular material stocks both grope for — how to test: compute Hill-number multifunctionality for a published structural-battery dataset AND for a published material-stock inventory using the same q; if both work, one metric spans engineering multifunctionality and material-stock diversity — effort: M`
- `Σ = P/(F·Δu) (C8) ↔ ecological energetics "trophic transfer efficiency" and free-energy harvest from environmental gradients — same object: power extracted divided by the product of a force and a velocity difference across the same reservoir pair; ecosystem energetics quantifies gradient dissipation (Schneider & Kay, exergy destruction) and never writes the bounded bilinear form — how to test: form Σ for a canonical gradient-harvesting organism (a soaring bird already in C8; then a chemolithotroph on a redox gradient) and check the [0,1] bound holds with the reservoir named — effort: M; this is also the natural bridge into astrobiology (see §3)`
- `C4 statocyte pooling bound ↔ conservation sensor networks / distributed detection theory — same object: minimum detectable signal scaling as M^(−1/2) under pooling vs M^(−1) under summation, identical to the sensor-fusion literature's fundamental limits — how to test: check whether distributed-detection theory already states the discriminator; if so C4's novelty grade drops, which is worth knowing cheaply — effort: S (this doubles as the prior-art kill-check the novelty audit says C4 needs)`

## Proposed frame paragraph

> This is a cross-domain gap-finding inquiry run under one rule: **a thread is in scope if it can reach real, checkable science.** The method is to find places where two fields quantify the *same physical object* in different vocabularies, never cross-cite, and therefore each hold half of a result — then either build the missing shared object or prove it cannot exist. The recurring objects are the ones that govern how living and engineered systems *persist*: how available a repairable thing is, how it fails as a distribution rather than a mean, how much repair its finite budget buys, what dose of stress strengthens it before it breaks, and when to abandon a depleting patch. Those are the physics of durability, maintenance, recovery and foraging — which is to say they are the physics that ecology, circularity and conservation are made of, and the physics any account of life elsewhere would also have to obey. The project therefore reads as biomimicry in the strict sense: not copying shapes, but transporting *quantified* design laws between biology, engineering and ecosystem-scale resource use, in whichever direction the gap runs. Its outputs are a method, a measured catalogue of absences, a handful of genuine bridges, and a visible column of self-corrections — and its next obligation is to stop locating gaps and start pushing one bridge until it emits a number someone else can check.
