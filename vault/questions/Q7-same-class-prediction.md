# The one result the selection bias cannot touch

> **Every closed same-class disagreement in the catalogue resolved to systematics. No
> exceptions, across seven cases.** That is a conditional, so it does not depend on the
> denominator — and it turns the fine structure constant into a **prediction**.

## Why this survives the bias that ruins everything else

[[C7-discrepancy-catalogue]] cannot give an honest base rate. Quiet convergence has no
publication genre, so systematics resolutions are systematically unfindable, and the famous
cases are famous for going the interesting way. The headline fraction is not trustworthy.

**But this claim is not a fraction.** It is *given same-class, then systematics*. Adding the
missing invisible cases can only add more systematics resolutions — the direction that
strengthens it. **The bias cannot cut against this one.**

That is the difference between a number that needs a denominator and a conditional that needs
only a counterexample. Nobody has found one.

## The prediction

The fine structure constant discrepancy is **same-class**: caesium and rubidium recoil, both
h/m atom interferometry, differing in implementation. Open at **5.4σ** with no third measurement.

> **It will resolve to systematics.** Not new physics. And the suspected culprit — wavefront
> curvature — is already named.

This is falsifiable, dated, and costs nothing to record. If a third measurement lands and the
resolution is a real effect, the conditional dies and takes this note with it.

## Why it matters that this makes α *less* interesting

[[fine-structure-discrepancy]] calls it "the sharpest live same-method disagreement in
metrology" — true, and the framing invites you to watch it for new physics. **The catalogue says
the opposite.** Same-class disagreements are where measurement error lives.

The interesting open cases are the **different-class** ones with an independent relation picking
a side. That reprioritises the whole watch list, and it is the first time this project's
catalogue has told us where *not* to look.

## An independent seam strengthened it, and found no counterexample

A separate metrology-and-constants pass, run without reference to this conditional, produced
**four more closed same-class cases. All four resolved to systematics:**

- **LKB Rb recoil, 2011 vs 2020, same laboratory**, 2.4σ → systematics in the 2011 result
  (photon momentum in a distorted optical field, Gouy phase, Doppler ramp). The cleanest
  which-side-was-wrong datum in the whole set.
- **Hydrogen spectroscopy, 2S–4P vs 1S–3S vs 2S–2P** → systematics, the large-r_p results.
- **Historical recommended values of c**, Birge ratio 1.47 → reported uncertainties too small.
- **Successive CODATA adjustments 2006→2017** → the prior adjustments' uncertainty statements.

**Seven for seven becomes eleven for eleven.** Still no counterexample.

The one same-class case that is still **open** is the tenth-order QED coefficient — two Monte
Carlo evaluations of the same number, **4.8σ apart**. That is a second live prediction: it
should also resolve to error in one calculation, and it is a cleaner test than α because no new
physics is even conceivable there.

## The open question

**How large is the same-class evidence base really?** Seven closed cases is a suggestive
conditional, not an established one. The same catalogue method, run for same-class cases
specifically rather than for famous disagreements, would settle it — and same-class cases are
*less* fame-biased than the general population, because they rarely make headlines either way.

That is a smaller, cleaner, more findable sample than the base rate ever was. **The narrow
question is more answerable than the broad one it came from.**
