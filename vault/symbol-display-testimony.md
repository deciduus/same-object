# A reported symbolic display, read as a specification

> Testimony about a UAP screen/display. Recorded under the claims-register rule —
> **testimony sets the specification, never the mechanism** — and clearly tiered. The value
> here is not the account; it is stating exactly what would make such an account checkable.
> Connects to [[Q10-substrate-independent-logic]] (the "coding language" thread).

## The testimony, as relayed

Tier: **TESTIMONIAL**, and the **provenance is contested** — a Lazar-type clearanced account,
relayed second- or third-hand, hedged "allegedly" by the user. Not corroborated by any sample.

What it specifies, faithfully:

- a display that **powers on** in a window of a craft;
- **moving symbols** — to a human eye *reminiscent of Korean* (blocky/featural), but **not a
  known language**;
- **artifacts in motion**: drifting and slanting, tilting forward and tipping backward on the
  X axis.

The user noted this "isn't a spec" — correct. It is a verbal impression, one remove further
from evidence than a sighting, because it is a memory of a *representation*.

## What testimony can and cannot establish here

- **Specification (testimony can carry this):** *that a dynamic glyph system with those visual
  and motion properties was reported.* That is a describable target.
- **Mechanism (testimony cannot carry this):** that the glyphs are a language, that they encode
  anything, that the account is authentic, or that the craft exists. None of that follows from
  the description, however vivid.

This is the [[reservoir-audit]] stance applied to symbols: not "real or fake," but *what would
have to be true, and what would discriminate it.*

## The discriminating test — if a sample ever existed

A verbal account is untestable. **A single clear image or a few video frames would not be** —
because three hypotheses for "moving symbols like Korean but unknown" leave different measurable
fingerprints:

| Hypothesis | Character inventory | Frequency law | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A real language / code** | bounded, recurring alphabet (Korean Hangul: ~24 jamo in blocks) | Zipfian rank-frequency; conditional entropy well below uniform | motion is **syntactic** — drift/tilt would correlate with glyph identity or sequence |
| **Motion-graphic decoration** | may be large or ad hoc; glyphs need not recur | flat / non-Zipfian; high entropy | motion is **decorative** — independent of glyph content, a UI flourish |
| **Procedural noise** | unbounded, few exact repeats | ~uniform | motion is the *only* structure; glyphs are filler |

So the checkable questions are concrete: **How large is the character inventory? Do glyphs
recur with a Zipfian frequency? Is the conditional entropy well below uniform (the signature of
a real code)? And critically — does the drift/tilt carry information (track glyph identity or
order) or is it decorative?** The last one is the sharpest: in a real writing system the motion
would be *syntactic or diacritic*; in a graphic it is ornament.

That converts "I heard about a display" into a falsifiable protocol — which is the only honest
way to hold it, and the same move that made [[Q10-substrate-independent-logic]] a countable
question instead of a metaphysical one.

## Status

**Recorded as testimony, not evidence.** No sample exists to run the test above; the account is
contested at the source. It earns a place only as a *specification with a discriminating
protocol attached* — if imagery is ever produced, the protocol is ready. Until then it is a
