# Latch-mediated spring actuation

> Muscle tops out near 500 W/kg. Decoupling slow loading from fast release beats it by orders
> of magnitude — and the **latch** is explicitly the least-characterized element in the field.

## The design rule that exists

Ilton et al. (2018, *Science* 360:eaao1082) give a genuine cascade of limits: below a critical
mass, **spring-driven beats motor-driven**, and a sequence of constraints — motor, spring,
latch, mass — determines which component binds. That is a real design rule from comparative
biology, not an inventory of fast animals.

## The numbers

| System | Performance |
|---|---|
| Muscle, ceiling | ~500 W/kg |
| Trap-jaw ant | 64 m/s in 0.13 ms, ~10^5 g |
| Mantis shrimp | 23–31 m/s, plus a **second impulse from cavitation collapse** |
| Fern sporangium | ~6300 g, loaded by **evaporation** at zero metabolic cost |

## What is missing

The **latch**. Springs and geometry are well modelled; the microsecond, low-friction,
repeatably-releasing contact is not. See [[G12-latch-fatigue]] — fatigue data exists for the
spring *material* and not for the latch contact.

Repeated trap-jaw strikes show **no decline in peak velocity**; fatigue appears in the muscle
motor. So the latch outlasts the measurement and nobody has run one to failure.

## Where it sits

The exemplar of [[M3-separate-timescales]]. The fern's escapement — fast elastic recoil
against slow viscous return, with no crossbar — is the purest case, and belongs equally to
[[M4-change-the-actor]].
