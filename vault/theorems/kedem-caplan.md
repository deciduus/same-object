# Kedem-Caplan degree of coupling

> Works for any two conjugate flux-force pairs. **Thermoelectric ZT is algebraically a special
> case:** q² = ZT/(1+ZT).

## Correction: this is not an unread theorem

**It was catalogued here as one, and that was wrong.** The full-text re-read of
[[G1-gradient-coupling]] found it in active use, not sitting unread:

- Chimal-Eguia et al., *Entropy* 25(12):1575 (2023),
  [PMC10743106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10743106/) — applies it to
  thermoelectric generators **and** oxidative phosphorylation in one document, cites Kedem &
  Caplan 1965 directly, and states the ZT relation in words.
- Electrokinetic energy conversion uses the same algebraic object, β = K₁₃²/(K₁₁K₃₃ − K₁₃²),
  traced to **Morrison & Osterle 1965** — a parallel derivation in the same year.
- arXiv:[2403.20209v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.20209v2) clones the form into a hydronic
  figure of merit, saying so explicitly.

The "**2 co-citers**" figure that put it on this list measured traffic between two named
papers. It did not measure whether the *result* had travelled. It had.

**This is the counting failure in its purest form**, and it damages the project's headline
pattern — *the unifying result exists, unread* — for this entry specifically. See
[[reading-not-counting]].

## What is actually true

The closed form `q² = ZT/(1+ZT)` is still not printed in the sources read. The connection is
stated qualitatively and used structurally; the one-line identity is not written down. That is
a much smaller claim than "unread," and it is the honest one.

**Limit, unchanged:** linear-response only. It cannot reach dynamic soaring (nonlinear,
trajectory-dependent) or solar sails (no conjugate flux pair). Extending it past linear
response remains the open problem in [[G1-gradient-coupling]], and that half of the gap
survived the re-read intact.
