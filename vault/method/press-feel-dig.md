# Press, feel, dig

> The user's observation, and the truest thing said about the method: the way we work a gap is
> the same shape as the instrument we built, which is the same shape as the engine the project
> started from. **Press on the thing, feel where it resists, dig where it gives.** The method
> mirrors the mechanism.

## The recurring shape

`macro → concept → gap → research → synthesis → application` — and it **recurses**: each
application opens a new macro. The project is not a pipeline with an end; it is this loop at
larger and larger radius. [[strategy]]'s three layers are the same loop viewed by reach.

## One move, three scales

| Scale | Press on | Feel the resistance | Dig for |
|---|---|---|---|
| The founding engine | "like gravity — an outward force" | it will not reduce to a force with no partner | the second reservoir → [[C8-momentum-harvesting-metric]] |
| The instrument | a conservation identity | `Σ > 1`, the books do not close | the misidentified reservoir → [[reservoir-audit]] |
| The research loop | a candidate gap | both sides calibrate, contact stays zero | the missing object, or the held-fixed constant → [[M6-vary-what-was-held-fixed]] |

Same move at every scale. That self-similarity is why an instrument built for the subject also
works *as* the method: [[specification-instruments]] is press-feel-dig with the middle two steps
made discrete.

## The WASD refinement — navigate by the resistance gradient

The user's sharper form, a gamer's analogy: **you don't move by choosing a direction. You tap
each button, feel whether you advance or hit a wall, and the *pattern* of resistance points you.**
WASD is not four decisions — it is a probe of the terrain, and the give tells you where to go.

Applied here: a node has a small set of **buttons** — the instruments, each a direction you can
press.

| Button | Press | Wall feels like | Give feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| calibrate both sides | do both literatures return real hits? | one side is empty — the gap is an artifact | both calibrate — the contact is real |
| [[citation-intersection]] | do any works cite both? | dozens do — it is connected | zero at good coverage — genuinely apart |
| construct the object | can the missing figure of merit be written? | a hidden parameter kills it ([[C15-metastability-metric]]) | it falls out ([[C6-damage-healing-ratio]]) |
| [[M6-vary-what-was-held-fixed]] | which held-fixed constant moves? | the bound reappears ([[C9-moving-coupling-point]]) | the constant releases |
| availability audit | does the reservoir/sink balance? | `Σ > 1`, excluded | it closes, or specifies the residual |

You press them all on the node and **read the gradient** — the direction that gives most, or
resists most informatively, is the way to dig. You do not pre-commit a heading; you feel the
field and follow it.

**This is the same move the project studies.** The subject is coupling to a gradient. The method
*is* coupling to a resistance gradient — pressing the instruments, reading the give, moving down
the path of most information. The map runs the territory's own algorithm. That is the deepest
layer of the self-similarity, and it is why [[specification-instruments]] feels native to the
subject rather than imposed on it.

## The tasking correction — the actionable part

**Hand an agent an object and a set of buttons, not a verb.** Let it press each direction,
report the resistance profile, and dig the way that gave.

- *Old (fragile):* "harden this / build this / kill this / fit this." The verb is chosen in
  advance. If the resistance disagrees, the agent either forces the wrong verb (a bad result) or
  stalls.
- *New (press-feel-dig):* "press on this node with the instruments; do whatever the resistance
  calls for." The verb — harden / build / narrow / kill / fit — is an **output**, read off the
  resistance, never an input.

**The evidence is in this project's own record.** The strongest results came from verb-agnostic
briefs that said *build it OR prove it cannot be built — either is a real result*:

- [[C9-moving-coupling-point]] — pressed, felt the bound hold, **killed** the device.
- [[C15-metastability-metric]] — pressed, felt the prefactor, **killed** the unifier, kept a salvage.
- [[C14-degree-of-passivity]] — pressed, felt a second axis, **narrowed** into a lattice.
- [[C6-damage-healing-ratio]], [[C8-momentum-harvesting-metric]], [[C12-pi-space-lattice]] — pressed, felt a real object, **built** it.

Every verb was chosen by the resistance. The briefs that pre-committed a single verb were the
weakest ones written.

## Why this is antifragile, again

A verb-assigned probe is fragile to surprise: it meets unexpected resistance and breaks. A
press-and-feel probe *gains* from surprise — the resistance is the information, and the
unexpected verb is often the better finding (C15's prefactor, C14's lattice, C9's twelve-year
prior art were all surprises worth more than the thing originally asked for). Same convex-to-
disorder property that makes [[specification-instruments]] antifragile rather than predictive.

## The standing brief shape

For future fan-outs: give each agent **(1) an object** — a gap, a claim, an identity — **(2) the
instruments** to press it with, and **(3) permission for every outcome** — closed, narrowed,
killed, hardened, inconclusive — with the explicit line that a clean negative or a
surprise-verb is a real result. Then let the resistance write the verb.
