# The fine structure constant discrepancy

> **Two atom-interferometry measurements of the same quantity, by the same class of
> technique, disagree at 5.4 sigma.** Unresolved as of 2026, with no third measurement.

Far less famous than it deserves, and the sharpest live instance of the pattern in
[[G17-overconfident-uncertainties]] and [[G9-discrepancy-base-rate]].

## Why it matters beyond itself

The electron g−2 measurement predicts alpha to **0.11 parts per billion** — roughly **ten
times smaller** than the caesium–rubidium gap. So the sharpest precision test in physics is
bottlenecked entirely on this disagreement. Resolve alpha and the Standard Model test improves
by an order of magnitude overnight.

## Why it is diagnostic

It is a **same-method** disagreement: both are h/m recoil atom interferometry, differing in
implementation. Per the discriminating features in [[G9-discrepancy-base-rate]]:

> When two implementations of one technique disagree, no new physics is on offer — only a
> shared-model error one side got wrong.

The suspected culprit is wavefront curvature. Compare the two gravitational-constant methods
in one laboratory disagreeing at 2.7 sigma, which was always the tell there.

## The company it keeps

| Quantity | Disagreement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Newton's constant G | ~13 sigma between methods | open, unmoved across two CODATA cycles |
| **Fine structure constant** | **5.4 sigma, same technique class** | **open, no third measurement** |
| Neutron lifetime | ~4.5 sigma | open; survived its best mundane explanation in 2025 |
| Proton radius | was ~7 sigma | resolved to systematics in the older method |
| Muon g−2 | was ~5 sigma | migrated into a theory-versus-theory dispute |
