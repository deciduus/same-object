# Precedent — this method has a name, and it is not ours

Checked deliberately. The result is deflationary and is carried forward rather than buried.

The method is **Literature-Based Discovery**, founded by Don Swanson in 1986. The title of
his 1987 paper is essentially this project's thesis statement:

> *Two medical literatures that are logically but not bibliographically connected.*

**Non-co-citation as evidence of a real gap is Swanson's founding move**, not our refinement.
He found fish oil for Raynaud's syndrome that way; it was confirmed in a clinical trial in
1989.

**We walked into our own [[failure-modes|synonym trap]].** This project ran literature-based
discovery all session without using the term. Querying the right name at the start would have
surfaced the precedent immediately.

## What honestly survives

| Feature | Status |
|---|---|
| cross-disciplinary rather than biomedical | **underexplored, not new** |
| co-citation as gap evidence | **fully standard.** Claim nothing |
| **quantified metric on both sides as an entry criterion** | **defensible** — no LBD method found gates on commensurable quantification |
| **[[positive-controls]]** | **a real contribution** |

## Why the controls matter more than expected

After forty years, LBD's canonical set of *experimentally validated* discoveries is roughly
**two**, both Swanson's own. Someone showed three published LBD "discoveries" were not valid
discoveries at all, having passed peer review. A 2023 *Bioinformatics* paper argues the field
"is built on sand" because its evaluation rests on cherry-picked replications.

Running known-closed pairs as controls — which this project started doing by accident —
answers that documented complaint directly.

**Honest positioning:** an instrument built *on top of* literature-based discovery, in its
thin non-biomedical branch, with two methodological additions.
