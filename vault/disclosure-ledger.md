# The disclosure ledger

> **What this is.** A sourced provenance ledger of the post-2017 US UAP disclosure thread,
> built to the project's claims-register discipline: **testimony sets the specification, never
> the mechanism** ([[reservoir-audit]], METHOD §3). Every entry is sorted into exactly one of
> three tiers — DOCUMENTED (a checkable public fact), TESTIMONIAL (a named person's on-record
> claim, not independently corroborated), ATTRIBUTED/REFUTED (a claim a later investigation
> explained) — and carries a confirmation status with a source. Every factual claim is either
> VERIFIED with a fetched URL this session or marked UNVERIFIED. This is documentarian work.
> **Credulity and dismissal are both failures here.** The ledger records what people said and
> what checked out; it never asserts in the project's voice what UAP *are*.
>
> **The one distinction the whole ledger turns on.** Two things get collapsed in public
> discussion and must be held apart: *"official secrecy and officially-unexplained sensor data
> were acknowledged"* — which **checked out** — and *"recovered non-human hardware/biologics
> exist"* — which **has not been corroborated and was affirmatively pushed back on by the
> government's own office**. Both are in the ledger. They are not the same claim.

## 1. Q1 lens — why do the strongest claims stay open?

[[Q1-what-gets-checked]] holds that **the cost and publishability of the check, not the importance
of the claim, predicts whether a disputed claim gets tested**. Apply it to the open rows.

- **Fravor / Graves / the 18 ODNI incidents (rows 3, 8, 9).** These are "checked and unresolved,"
  but unresolved for a Q1-shaped reason the ODNI report states outright: the sensors that caught
  them were "not generally suited for identifying UAP," and no standardized reporting existed
  before 2019. The check that would resolve them — purpose-built, calibrated multi-sensor
  collection — is *resource-intensive* (the report says so) and, unlike refuting DAMA, earns no
  one a clean publishable result. So the data sit in an "other" bin. This is the DAMA/Homestake
  split of Q1: the apparatus to check is expensive and the refutation is not obviously
  career-making, so it does not get built. AARO's GREMLIN sensor program is, in effect, the field
  finally trying to lower that check-cost.
- **Grusch's crash-retrieval/biologics claim (row 7).** This is the opposite failure: it is
  **unfalsifiable-as-stated to an outside checker**, because the specifics are classified and
  routed through claimed access that cannot be independently entered. Per Q1's corollary, an
  unchecked claim of this shape is *weak evidence of anything* — and here it was in fact checked
  by the one body with subpoena-and-clearance access (AARO, row 11), which reported back no
  corroboration. The claim stays "open" in public discourse mainly because its check is not
  publicly repeatable, not because the evidence is balanced.

The Q1 reading is clean: **the officially-unexplained sensor cases are unresolved because the
confirming check is unfunded/expensive; the exotic-recovery claims are unresolved because they are
structured to be uncheckable by anyone outside the compartment — and where an insider check did
run, it came back negative.**

## 2. Reservoir-audit / claims-register rule — specification vs mechanism

The [[reservoir-audit]] and METHOD §3 discipline: **testimony sets the specification (what was
observed/reported), never the mechanism (what it was).** Sorting the ledger on that line:

**SPECIFICATIONS testimony can legitimately establish** (what was reported, as an observable):
- "An object was tracked across radar, IR, and visual, appearing to move without visible
  propulsion" (ODNI rows 3, 8) — this is a row in a claims register: an observable with sensors
  named. It is exactly the "excludes matter at ~1 m" kind of entry METHOD §3 admits.
- "There exists USG UAP data not publicly released" (Mellon, row 18) — a specification about the
  *reporting*, and a credible one.
- "I was told a recovery program exists" (Grusch, row 7) — this establishes only the
  specification *that the assertion was made and by whom*, nothing about the referent.

**MECHANISM claims testimony cannot establish, no matter the credential:**
- "The objects are non-human craft" / "non-human biologics were recovered" (rows 7, 20) — these
  name *what it is*. A credentialed witness saying it does not move it out of the mechanism
  column. This is the precise error the reservoir audit was built to refuse: converting a
  specification ("a reported recovery") into a verdict ("it is alien").

The registry stays valid regardless of how any account is later judged, because the rows are about
observables and provenance, not about who is trustworthy.

## 3. Selection bias — stated as prominently as the ledger

**Credentialed testimony that reaches media and Congress is not a random sample, and a credential
is not evidence for the claim.**

Every name in the TESTIMONIAL tier passed through the same filter: they chose to go public,
found a receptive committee or outlet, and had a credential that made them bookable. That filter
selects *hard* on people who believe something striking and are willing to say it — and selects
*out* the (unknowable in number) credentialed insiders with the same access who saw nothing
remarkable, or who declined to speak. We have no denominator. The ledger cannot tell you how many
NRO/NGA/Navy officers with Grusch-equivalent clearance concluded there was nothing to report,
because non-reporting is invisible — the same structural blindness METHOD §13 flags ("publication
deletes the failures").

**What a credential does and does not carry, stated honestly:**
- It **does** establish the person is not a random crank: they held the clearances, the flight
  hours, the office. Fravor really flew the intercept; Mellon really oversaw special-access
  programs; Grusch really was the NRO's rep to the task force. That is not nothing — it rules out
  fabrication-of-self.
- It **does not** transfer to the claim. A rear admiral's certainty about non-human intelligence
  (row 20) is epistemically a MECHANISM claim from a single witness, and METHOD §5's rule applies
  unchanged: *single-group/single-witness claims resolve against the claimant absent an
  independent, orthogonal check.* The uniform, the oath, and the sincerity do not raise the prior
  on the referent; they only raise the prior that the person believes it.

The honest weight: **a credential moves a claim from "ignore" to "log and specify." It does not
move it toward "true."**

### Bias runs both ways — the symmetry the user insisted on

The selection filter above is a bias in the **testimony lane**. It would be dishonest to name
that and not name the symmetric bias in the **academic/institutional lane**, because treating
peer-reviewed-funded-published work as automatically decisive is *also* a bias — one this project
has already documented against itself:

- [[Q1-what-gets-checked]]: what gets checked is set by the *cost and publishability* of the
  check, not the importance of the claim. The academic filter tracks economics, not truth.
- [[Q8-the-invisible-population]]: the largest body of scientific disagreement is never published
  *as* disagreement — it is absorbed into consensus estimators and expansion factors.
- The four confirmed unread theorems and the project's own 578-reference error: fields not reading
  each other, and a stale number propagating unchecked through five files.

So the honest frame is **not** "documentary lane trustworthy, testimony lane biased." It is:
*every lane carries a characteristic bias, and none earns 100% trust.* The academic lane is
strong on reproducible mechanism and blind to the unfunded, unpublishable, and cross-domain. The
documentary lane has strong provenance and is biased by what an institution chooses to release
and how it frames it (AARO's own framing is contested, row 16). The testimony lane can carry a
real firsthand observable and is biased by self-selection and narrative. **The referee across all
of them is the same:** does an independent, orthogonally-biased lane converge on the *same
checkable observable*? That is the signal — not authority within any one lane. See
[[evidence-lanes]].

## 4. Does it "check out years later"? — the user's observation, tested honestly

Split it into the two claims that get collapsed, and grade each against the ledger.

**Version A — "institutional secrecy and officially-unexplained sensor data were real, and got
acknowledged." This CHECKED OUT.**
- A secret Pentagon UAP program (AATIP) was denied-by-omission and then *confirmed to exist*
  (row 1).
- Videos dismissed as hoaxes were *officially authenticated and released* by DoD, and formally
  labeled "unidentified" (row 2).
- The intelligence community itself put on the record that **143 of 144 cases were unexplained**
  and 18 showed apparently anomalous kinematics (row 3).
- Congress built a standing office, passed reporting law, and held sworn hearings (rows 4, 6, 10,
  14, 21, 22).
- **2026 PURSUE (rows 23–24)** released declassified astronaut debriefing transcripts as primary
  records — moving a set of Apollo-era "unexplained lights" accounts from lore to *documented
  primary source*. This adds to Version A: more officially-released, officially-unexplained
  observation data. It is **observation, not recovered materiel**, so it is silent on Version B.
  Someone who said in 2017 "there is a real, hidden, government UAP effort and there is sensor
  data the government can't explain" was **vindicated by the documentary record.** That is the
  defensible core of the user's observation, and it is genuinely non-trivial.

**Version B — "recovered non-human hardware and biologics exist." This did NOT get established —
and is the one place the government's own office pushed back.**
- The crash-retrieval/biologics claim entered the record **explicitly secondhand** (row 7).
- The body with the clearance and the subpoena to check it reported **no evidence** for the
  reverse-engineering narrative (row 11).
- The specific artifacts offered as proof were attributed to mundane causes: the alleged material
  was ordinary/terrestrial (row 12); the alleged cover-program (KONA BLUE) was a never-approved
  paper SAP (row 13).
  This half has *not* trended toward confirmation over the years. If anything the trend since 2023
  is the opposite: the more the specific recovery claims were checked, the more they attributed to
  prosaic causes — while the claims that survive survive precisely where they remain unchecked
  (the Q1 point).

**The verdict, undivided into its two halves would be misleading. Divided:** the *secrecy and
unexplained-data* thesis checked out; the *recovered-alien-materiel* thesis did not, and was
actively contradicted by the one investigation empowered to look. Collapsing them — treating the
vindication of Version A as if it were evidence for Version B — is the single most common
reasoning error in this whole topic, and the reservoir-audit rule (§2 above) is exactly the tool
that keeps them apart. This is the same structure as [[Q10-substrate-independent-logic]]'s honest
handling of "the impossibility column keeps shrinking": what shrank was the *unwarranted
dismissal of officially-unexplained data*, not the *evidentiary gap under the exotic claim*.
