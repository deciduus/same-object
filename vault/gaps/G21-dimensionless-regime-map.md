# No unified dimensionless regime map

**STANDING: NARROWED** · evidence: full-text-read · contact surface: 1 · last checked 2026-09-05

> **Surviving half closed by construction, 2026-09-03: [[C12-pi-space-lattice]].** The absent
> object was "one Π-space co-locating organisms and processes." Buckingham's theorem makes that
> the integer kernel of a dimension matrix, and building one — organism quantities and process
> quantities in a single 3×10 matrix — puts Froude, Reynolds, Péclet and Damköhler in **one
> rank-7 lattice.** The space exists and is constructible. The residual is no longer "it is
> absent" but "nobody had built it": the co-location manufactures no coincident-transition
> organism (Froude and Péclet coincide at L≈0.9 µm where Re≈10⁻³ and Froude is not live), which
> is itself the finding — the two families occupy disjoint operative regions.

> No single framework places organisms **and processes** in dimensionless-number space so that
> two different groups' crossovers can be read against each other.

## The previous narrowing rested on typography, and is withdrawn

The note had narrowed the claim to *"no **tabulated** regime map with crossover conditions."*
**That word was doing illegitimate work.** Vogel states crossover conditions repeatedly and
numerically (locators in the Provenance block below — all three are from the 1998 *Physics Today* feature,
not from either book):

> "Going faster than the critical Froude number of about 0.16 requires that the ship leave its
> beneficent stern wave astern"

> "all will reach the practical amplitude maximum at about the same Froude number, which turns
> out to be between 0.5 and 0.6"

> "Alexander noted as well that the trot-to-gallop transition for quadrupeds occurs at Froude
> numbers between 2 and 4"

Plus Bond < 1 and a capillary-rise ceiling near 3 m. **"It is in prose, not in a table" is not
a research gap.** The concept *crossover condition* is unambiguously present in the very source
the note cites. A claim that survives only on formatting is not a claim.

## What survives, and it is the substantive half

Vogel is a **sequence of separate one-group arguments**, each with its own critical value and
no common space. Checked ABSENT in the full text: **the Péclet number is never mentioned**, and
there is no table or figure placing organisms or processes on shared dimensionless axes. Its
figures are individual phenomena — rubber ducks, midges, blood pressure against height.

So there is no axis on which a Froude transition and a Péclet transition sit together. **That
is the gap**, and it is about a missing shared *space*, not a missing *number*.

Corroborating from the other direction: a 2024 PNAS Nexus paper does exactly this for pedestrian
crowds. The object exists as a genre in an adjacent field, which **strengthens** the absence in
biology rather than weakening it.

## Restated

*Crossover values are published per-group and in prose. What is absent is a single Π-space in
which organisms and processes are co-located, so that two groups' crossovers can be read
against each other.*

Also: `"Buckingham"` is contaminated by a developmental biologist's surname and must never be
used as a search handle. See [[homographs]].

## The identity worth keeping

The maximum-cell-size question and the Peclet number are the
same equation. Diffusion time goes as x^2/D — 1 um takes 0.01 s, 1 cm takes two weeks. Motors run
at ~1 um/s, so transport beats diffusion above ~10 um, and **that crossover is Pe = 1.**

### What this does to the `full-text-read` grade

The "read in full" was a **six-page magazine feature**, not a 400-page textbook. The ABSENT check
was re-run against that article, 2026-09-05, and **holds on its own terms**: the article names
Froude, Bond, Weber, Reynolds, the Bernoulli–Hagen–Poiseuille ratio and Froude propulsion
efficiency, **never the Péclet number**, and contains **no table or figure placing organisms and
processes on shared dimensionless axes**. So the finding stands — but it was measured on a much
smaller object than the note implied, and a full-text ABSENT check against *Life in Moving
Fluids* 2nd ed. and *Comparative Biomechanics* 2nd ed. has **never been run**.
