# Resize versus throttle

**STANDING: OVERTURNED — do not cite as a gap** · evidence: full-text-read · contact surface: 5 · last checked 2026-09-03

> Textbook case of **anchoring on the originating field's term.** The claim was that
> symmorphosis never reached computing or mechanical engineering. Both halves are false.

## Computing has the question, with formulas

arXiv:[1905.10270](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10270), read in full, defines
**over-provisioning accuracy**:

`a_O = (1/TR) · Σ max(s_t − d_t, 0)`

described as *"the average fraction of resources that the autoscaler supplies in excess of the
current demand"*, together with over- and under-provisioning **time share**.

That is the symmorphosis question — how much spare capacity, and how much of the time — in
computing's own vocabulary, quantified. **The word "safety" occurs zero times in the paper.**
Anchoring on *symmorphosis* and *safety factor* measured the words, not the concept.

## And mechanical engineering is where it came from

Safety factors and fully stressed design are **native** to mechanical engineering.
Symmorphosis borrowed *from* it. The note claimed the formalism "never reached mechanical
engineering," which **inverts the direction of travel.** See [[one-way-borrowing]] — export
direction tells you nothing about import direction, and here the export direction itself was
recorded backwards.

## What might still survive, unverified

Only the **cost and latency of a capacity change** — biology pays in ATP and weeks, engineering
in capex and years, with no shared axis. **That axis was not checked** and this entry is not
evidence for it. If it is worth pursuing it should be opened as a new entry with its own test,
not carried on this one's corpse.

## Worth keeping

Two severe homographs found here: **"autoscaling" is owned by chemometrics**, and **"rate
limiting" by biochemistry.** See [[homographs]]. The chemometrics sense is probably part of why
the computing literature stayed invisible.
