Part I — The organizing principle

Four moves that keep winning

Extracted from seven literature sweeps across physics, biology, and instrumentation. None of them is "build a stronger thing."

These recurred independently across fields that do not cite one another. That is the useful finding — not any single result, but that separate disciplines each discovered the same handful of structural moves and none of them named it.

Move 01

Manufacture contrast

When the signal sits under the noise floor, don't amplify it. Create a local disparity that survives the floor.

A plant cell cannot feel its own weight — so it grows a dense starch grain whose displacement costs a few kBT. LIGO cannot make the wave bigger — so it squeezes the vacuum and relocates the noise.

Statoliths · squeezed light · magnetosome chains · dense-particle sensing

Move 02

Use the noise

The engineering reflex is to suppress it. Repeatedly wrong. Transport efficiency and detection thresholds are often non-monotonic in noise, with an optimum well above zero.

A statolith pile should jam below a yield angle. Cellular agitation fluidizes it into a continuous angle sensor. Quantum transport peaks at nonzero dephasing, confirmed on three hardware platforms.

ENAQT · active granular fluidization · stochastic resonance · self-tuned criticality

Move 03

Separate the timescales

Don't build a faster actuator. Decouple slow loading from fast release, and let an instability do the work.

Muscle tops out near 500 W/kg; latch-and-spring beats it by orders of magnitude. The fern sporangium needs no crossbar at all — fast elastic recoil and slow viscous return differ enough to act as an escapement, loaded by evaporation at zero metabolic cost.

LaMSA latches · snap-buckling · cavitation catapults · poroelastic bypass

Move 04

Change what does the work

When a theorem or a scaling law blocks the direct route, swap the category of the thing doing the work rather than pushing harder on it.

A mechanical mirror cannot move fast enough for the dynamical Casimir effect — so make the boundary a boundary condition with no mass, and win by twelve orders of magnitude. You cannot spin a medium fast enough for superradiance — so modulate it in time.

Circuit DCE · Floquet rotation · time-varying media · geometry as material

The common thread is worth stating precisely, because it is the operational form of "innovate like nature." Every one of these is a structural move, not a magnitude move. Engineering culture defaults to raising signal or lowering noise — both magnitude. Evolution cannot turn up the power, so it searches arrangement instead, which is why it keeps finding these first.

Part II — The register

Three specifications, entered

Each behavior stated as an observable, with every candidate mechanism that produces it, its real magnitude, and the measurement that separates candidates.

Spec 01 Not achievable as stated

A field that excludes matter — a region you cannot reach into

Candidate mechanisms

Flux pinning in a type-II superconductor

The closest real experience to an invisible barrier. Quantized vortices pin to material defects, so any translation or rotation costs condensation energy — it resists tilt, unlike ordinary magnetic levitation. Feels like the object is locked into empty space.

10–30 N/cm²
stiffness 1–20 N/mm
needs T < 92 K, cm gaps

Diamagnetic levitation

The only mechanism here that excludes all ordinary matter, because everything has a diamagnetic component. This is the levitating-frog experiment. But it works inside a bore, not in open air.

B·∇B ≈ 1400 T²/m
≈ 16 T in a 32 mm bore
bismuth ≈ 20× easier

Acoustic levitation / acoustophoretic traps

Real and increasingly sophisticated — single-sided holographic traps can hold and steer dozens of particles. But trapped objects must be smaller than half a wavelength, and the pressure is minuscule.

≈ 50 Pa at 165 dB
= 5 × 10⁻³ N/cm²
objects ≲ 4 mm at 40 kHz

Optical trapping

Nobel-winning, and it does not scale — not for engineering reasons but because of the momentum content of light itself. Holding up 100 grams would need ~300 MW, which ablates the object first.

3.34 nN per watt
hard ceiling = P/c
forces 0.1–100 pN

Plasma window

The strongest real case, and genuinely surprising: a wall-stabilized arc holds one atmosphere against high vacuum with no solid window. Arc heating raises viscosity and drops density, choking the flow. But it lives in a cooled channel — and you can put a screwdriver through it.

1 atm vs 10⁻⁵ torr
~10,000–15,000 K
tens of kW, cm aperture

Shockwave attenuation by transient ionized air

A granted patent, physically plausible: sensor-triggered arc heating creates an impedance mismatch that refracts a blast wave. Note carefully what it does not claim — it stops pressure waves, not projectiles. No public demonstration.

csound ∝ √T
pressure waves only
undemonstrated publicly

Discriminating test — material dependence. Present the same barrier with four probes: PTFE, aluminium, bismuth, and water. Diamagnetic force scales as χ/ρ, so bismuth responds ~18× more strongly than water. Eddy-current repulsion acts only on conductors. Acoustic force follows acoustic contrast, not conductivity. Gas-dynamic pressure is material-independent.

One probe set separates every candidate above. If the barrier acts equally on all four and no large field is present, none of these mechanisms is operating.

Spec 02 Achievable — three mechanisms land at 20°

A ~20° rotation of one component switches the main effect on or off

Note the spec: rotation as a state change, not as a power source. That distinction matters — it removes the magnitude problem entirely, because the rotation gates something already present rather than supplying it. What the spec demands is sharp angular selectivity: full output to near-zero across roughly 20°.

Candidate mechanisms

Multipole permanent-magnet flux switch

The best all-round fit. A rotor ring of alternating poles over a matching keeper gives coupling that goes as cos(Nφ), so the null sits at 90°/N. Five pole pairs lands at 18°, four at 22.5°. Zero power, latching, mechanical detents at each state, high force density. This is how you build a wire-free rotational switch.

output ∝ cos(Nφ)
N = 5 → null at 18°
N = 4 → null at 22.5°

Half-wave plate between crossed polarizers

An exact 22.5° switch, and beautifully clean. Zero output at 0°, full output at 22.5°, back to zero at 45°. Passive, contactless, no electronics. Extinction is limited only by polarizer quality. Applies only if the gated effect is optical.

I = I₀ sin²(2θ)
max at exactly 22.5°
extinction 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶

Stoner–Wohlfarth latched switching

The sharpest of the three, and the only true step. The field needed to flip a magnetic element depends on its angle to the applied field along the "astroid" curve — and it is easiest at 45°, a factor-two reduction. Hold the drive at 0.65 of the anisotropy field: at 0° it cannot switch, at 20° it does. Bistable, so the state persists after you let go.

h(0°) = 1.00
h(20°) = 0.574
h(45°) = 0.500 (min)

Magnetic gear / coupling near pull-out

Gates mechanical power rather than a field. Transmitted torque goes as sin(NΔθ) until the poles slip. Nine pole pairs puts the torque peak at 10° and the null at 20°. Robust, but the "off" state slips noisily rather than sitting quiet.

T = T_max sin(NΔθ)
N = 9 → null at 20°
then hysteretic slip

Nested Halbach cylinders — ruled out

The intuitive guess, and wrong. Two concentric arrays give a field that varies with the half-angle, so it is a smooth 180° dial with a single null per revolution. Excellent dynamic range (measured 1.47 T down to under 0.1 T) and near-zero rotation torque — but a 20° turn changes the field by 1.5%, not by everything.

B ∝ |cos(φ/2)|
20° → 1.5% change
null at 180°

Optical phase matching and Bragg — ruled out the other way

These fail by being far too sharp. A 1 cm doubling crystal is dead at ±0.04°; perfect-crystal Bragg selectivity is arcseconds. Volume holographic gratings are the loosest at roughly 3°, and pushing wider breaks the Bragg condition entirely. A witness would describe a knife-edge, not a detent.

phase matching ≈ 0.05°
crystal Bragg ≈ 0.002°
volume grating floor ≈ 3°

Discriminating test — turn it both ways, then fit the curve. Record the output against angle over a full revolution at one-degree steps, in both rotation directions.

Direction first. Cosine, sinc² and Darwin forms are all reversible and single-valued. Stoner–Wohlfarth switching and magnetic pole-slip are hysteretic — the on-angle and off-angle differ. A latching switch with different thresholds each way is bistable magnetic or mechanical, not optical or diffractive.

Then read the exponent. Fit cosn(kφ) and recover k. k = ½ means nested Halbach, and the 20° figure is then wrong by a factor of nine. k = 2 means a half-wave plate. k = 4–5 means a multipole flux switch — and you will also see that many equal nulls per revolution, with detent torque at each one.

Spec 03 Achievable — and unremarkable

A localized focal point that visibly bends light in open air

Candidate mechanisms

Plasma — and the sign is diagnostic

Free electrons drive the refractive index below 1, so an overdense plasma is a diverging lens. This is the opposite of ordinary refraction and it is the single most useful fact in this spec. Fully ionized air perturbs light ~12× more strongly than air itself, with the opposite sign.

n = √(1 − ωp²/ω²) < 1
ionized air: n ≈ 0.996
refractivity ≈ −4 × 10⁻³

Thermal / density gradient

The mundane, high-prior candidate. Any hot object, arc, or exhaust produces a visible free-floating distortion by this alone. It is what a mirage is. A 700 K plume perturbs the index as much as air's entire refractivity.

dn/dT ≈ −1 × 10⁻⁶ /K
700 K plume → Δn ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁴
slow ~Hz shimmer

Kerr self-focusing and filamentation

Dramatic and unmistakable: a thin, straight, free-floating glowing filament that can run for metres to kilometres, often with a coloured conical halo. Beautifully self-consistent with the row above — it is the plasma's sub-unity index that arrests the collapse and clamps the intensity.

Pcr ≈ 3–10 GW at 800 nm
clamps at 5 × 10¹³ W/cm²
≈ 100 µm core

Gradient-index metamaterial

Real, and the deepest version of geometry-as-material — a coordinate transformation maps directly onto a prescribed permittivity. But it requires a fabricated solid structure and is typically narrowband. Nothing free-floating.

requires a physical device
narrowband
optical demos sub-wavelength

Discriminating test — image a distant grid through the region, and take a spectrum. Diverging distortion with no rising plume and no buoyancy asymmetry points to plasma. Diverging or converging with a plume and slow shimmer points to thermal. A sharp filamentary line with a coloured halo points to a Kerr filament — and implies a terawatt-class laser in the line of sight.

The spectrum settles it outright: nitrogen second-positive bands at 337, 357 and 380 nm plus the oxygen line at 777 nm identify air plasma unambiguously. Broadband supercontinuum identifies filamentation. A blackbody continuum identifies plain incandescence.

Spec 04 Achievable — and it yields a number

Radio at one frequency passes through where other bands are blocked

This is the best row in the register, because it converts a qualitative report into a measurable quantity. Frequency-selective transmission is not unusual — it is the defining optical property of any plasma, and it has a hard edge rather than a gradual rolloff.

Candidate mechanisms

Plasma cutoff

Below the plasma frequency, radio reflects. Above it, radio passes. The transition is sharp, and the frequency is set entirely by electron density. This single effect explains the whole reported behavior.

fp ≈ 9000 √ne Hz
(ne in cm⁻³)

The everyday case — the ionosphere

You already depend on this. Shortwave bounces around the world because it sits below cutoff; FM and television go straight up into space because they sit above it. Same medium, opposite behavior, decided purely by frequency.

ne ≈ 10⁶ cm⁻³
cutoff ≈ 9 MHz

The engineering case — reentry blackout

A returning spacecraft builds a plasma sheath that kills communications. The standard mitigation is to move up in frequency — higher bands punch through where lower ones cannot. That is precisely the reported observation, in a well-documented setting.

blocks S-band
Ka-band penetrates
routine, documented

Cavity or aperture cutoff

A separate mechanism with a similar signature. Any enclosure passes frequencies above a cutoff set by its dimensions and blocks those below — this is why a microwave oven's door mesh is opaque to 2.45 GHz and transparent to visible light. Geometry alone can produce band selectivity.

set by aperture size
λ ≳ 2a → blocked
no plasma required

Discriminating test — sweep the frequency and find the edge. A receiver and a signal generator locate the cutoff directly, and the cutoff is the measurement. Read the electron density straight off it:

90 MHz → ~10⁸ cm⁻³  ·  900 MHz → ~10¹⁰  ·  9 GHz → ~10¹²

To separate plasma cutoff from geometric cutoff: plasma cutoff moves as conditions change and disappears when the source is off; aperture cutoff is fixed by the hardware and stays put. One frequency sweep in each state settles it.

Flag on this row: plasma cutoff is undergraduate material, in every plasma-physics and antenna textbook. An account in which trained physicists find this surprising is telling you something about the account, not about the physics.

Spec 05 Weakly diagnostic — but it fixes a scale

Nested geometry: a small source at the focus of a horn inside a dome

Reported shape is a weak constraint on its own — many things are cone-inside-dome. But geometry does one strongly useful thing: it fixes a characteristic length, and a length predicts a frequency. That turns a description into arithmetic.

What the shape buys

A cone with a cap is a horn

The standard geometry for turning something small and localized into directional radiation — horn antennas, acoustic horns, flux concentrators. If the design intent is a directional emitter, this is the shape you would draw.

throat sets cutoff
mouth sets gain

A dome around a source is a reflector or a cavity

It either returns things toward the centre or resonates. A dense object at the focus of a reflective shell is a recognizable arrangement across several fields — optical, acoustic, and nuclear.

resonates at λ ≈ 2d
d = dome diameter

The size-to-band calculation

The testable consequence, derived from nothing but reported dimensions. A metre-scale dome resonates in the low hundreds of megahertz. A dinner-plate dome resonates in the gigahertz. This is arithmetic, not inference, and it connects directly to Spec 04.

1 m → ~150 MHz
0.25 m → ~600 MHz
0.05 m → ~3 GHz

Discriminating test — check the reported band against the reported size. If a device of stated dimension d is described as emitting or interacting at a frequency, that frequency should sit near c/2d for a cavity, or above the horn's throat cutoff for an emitter. Agreement is weak evidence the description is internally consistent. Disagreement by orders of magnitude is a genuine inconsistency in the account, and costs nothing to check.

On the fuel claim specifically: "a stable superheavy isotope at the focus" has no candidate mechanism — the longest measured moscovium half-life is 0.65 seconds, roughly a hundred atoms have ever existed, and no model predicts a stable Z = 115 nuclide. The functional spec underneath it survives and is the row worth keeping: a small dense source at the focus of a horn inside a reflective shell. That geometry has real physics attached regardless of what sits in the middle.

Part III — Why Spec 01 fails

The exponent that closes the door

Not an engineering limit. A consequence of how fields couple to neutral matter.

Every non-contact force on neutral matter is second-order

Neutral matter has no monopole coupling to any field. You must induce a dipole first, and then act on it with a gradient. So the force is second-order in the field, and it depends on the gradient of the square:

F = ∇(α|E|²/2)   or   F = ∇(χV B²/2μ₀)

Now compound that with geometry. A field from any localized source falls at least as fast as a dipole, B ∝ 1/r³. Feed that into the expression above and the force on neutral matter falls as:

F ∝ 1/r⁷

That exponent is the whole story. The 1400 T²/m needed to levitate ordinary matter at 3 cm inside a bore becomes roughly 10⁻² T²/m at 3 metres — a shortfall of about five orders of magnitude, and you cannot recover it by raising B, because you would exceed material limits everywhere near the source long before.

The two escape routes both close. Radiation pressure is bounded by momentum flux at 3.3 nN per watt. Acoustic pressure is bounded by the onset of shock in air, and needs a standing wave — meaning a second surface, meaning a cavity, meaning not free space.

Everything with real stiffness requires placing a special material in the field: a superconductor, a strong diamagnet. Which means the exclusion zone is a property of the pairing, never of the field alone.

What this does and does not settle

It does not say a reported behavior is false. It says that if a matter-excluding barrier operates at standoff distance in open air at ambient temperature, it is not doing so by any known field-matter coupling — which makes it the single load-bearing claim in the whole description, and the one worth designing a measurement around.

That is the register working correctly. Two of three specs reduced to ordinary physics, so those observables carry no information and you stop spending attention on them. One did not reduce, and is now stated sharply enough to test.

Part III½ — The seed principle

Stored energy is cheap. Release control is expensive.

The generalized version of "a small dense package holding disproportionate energy" — and the reason the stability tradeoff is far softer than intuition says.

Strip the specific fuel claim and the underlying concept is metastability: a system resting in a deep local minimum behind a tall barrier. Stable indefinitely, until something lowers the barrier. This exists at every scale, and the quantitative relation governing all of it is a single expression.

Why energy density and stability are nearly independent

Lifetime against escape from a metastable state follows the Arrhenius form. At room temperature kT ≈ 2.5 kJ/mol, so:

τ ≈ τ₀ · exp(ΔG‡/kT)  →  every ~6 kJ/mol buys 10× lifetime

Which means a one-year lifetime needs a barrier of roughly 100 kJ/mol, and a million-year lifetime needs roughly 135 kJ/mol. Only 35% more barrier for a million times the endurance.

The barrier requirement grows logarithmically with lifetime, while energy density has no upper bound. So the intuitive tradeoff — more stored energy must mean less stable — is much weaker than it feels. They are close to independent parameters.

Diamond makes this concrete. Its driving force to become graphite is a mere 2.9 kJ/mol, while the barrier to actually doing it is 370–540 kJ/mol. Estimated room-temperature conversion time: about 10⁷⁰ seconds, against a universe roughly 10¹⁷ seconds old. Tiny driving force, enormous barrier, and the two numbers have nothing to do with each other.

Read the fat row against the TNT row. Fat carries roughly eight times TNT's energy and sits at 37 °C in living tissue for decades. The difference is not energy and not really barrier height — it is that TNT carries its own oxidizer. Its reaction is intramolecular, so it is not rate-limited by delivering oxygen and can propagate supersonically. Fat waits on your bloodstream. That is a difference in power density, not energy density, and it is the distinction that matters for design.

And the scoreboard on triggering is the real lesson. The two nuclear champions hold 10⁴–10⁶ times more energy than anything chemical and neither can be opened. Hafnium's triggered release was claimed in 1999, funded at roughly $30M, and refuted by an independent experiment at 100,000× the original sensitivity; Congress zeroed the program. Thorium-229 succeeded in 2024 for the opposite reason — its barrier is only 8.4 eV up, low enough that a laser can simply reach it.

The design principle, stated properly. Biology almost never releases stored energy by adding energy. It lowers the barrier — and a catalyst changes only ΔG‡, never ΔG. The magnitudes are staggering: one decarboxylase enzyme takes a reaction with an uncatalyzed half-life of 78 million years and completes it in 18 milliseconds, a rate enhancement of about 1017, using no metals and no cofactors.

A hand warmer is the purest illustration. Flexing the metal disc adds essentially zero energy. It supplies a surface — a nucleation site. The barrier is bypassed rather than climbed.

One necessary complication: biology also raises barriers deliberately, which is what dormancy is. So the accurate formulation is stronger than the one-directional version — biology treats barrier height as an independently tunable control variable in both directions, decoupled from the energy itself.

The most transferable trick is not a barrier at all. Tardigrades and bacterial spores vitrify — the cytoplasm becomes a glass at around 10¹² Pa·s, which arrests every degradative reaction simultaneously. It reverses because nothing was covalently changed: add water, the glass transition drops below ambient, and the whole system restarts at once. Not one tall barrier. A global mobility switch. Bacterial spores go from germinant binding to full rehydration in minutes, with sub-second commitment, and require no ATP to start — because they have essentially none.

An unclaimed opening

A dedicated search for cross-domain work on this found none. Energetic materials, nuclear isomers, phase-change storage and biological dormancy each have a mature literature quantifying their own stability–performance tradeoff. None of them cite each other, and there is no accepted figure of merit combining stored energy density, barrier height relative to kT, and triggerability.

The pieces are all published. The synthesis is not. That is the same pattern as the four moves in Part I — separate fields independently working one principle, with nobody naming it across the boundary.

Part III¾ — Cycle life

The carrier is a bus, not a tank

Human energy release is largely one-shot. Biology's is a loop. But the reason is not the one you would guess.

Burn a hydrocarbon and the carrier is destroyed. Biology almost never does this — and the natural explanation, "biological catalysts aren't consumed," turns out to be wrong. The real mechanism is more interesting and more transferable.

Two corrections that make the idea stronger

Enzymes are consumed. A 2021 study defined exactly this metric — catalytic cycles until replacement — and measured it across roughly 180 central-metabolism enzymes in three organisms. The range is 10³ to 10⁷ turnovers, with medians around 30,000–40,000 in yeast and bacteria. Then the enzyme is scrapped.

And the usual killer is not heat or wear. It is mechanism-based inactivation — the enzyme is damaged by the reaction it catalyzes. Some chemistries are simply more destructive to their own catalyst than others.

So biology's strategy is not an immortal catalyst. It is a cheap catalyst plus a continuous replacement line — an economic solution, not a durability one. Photosystem II makes this explicit: it replaces its own damaged subunit roughly every 30 minutes rather than attempting to build something that lasts.

Rate and lifetime are nearly decoupled. Carbonic anhydrase runs up to 1.4 million reactions per second. At that rate, a lifetime budget of 10⁷ cycles is ten seconds of work. Speed and endurance are separate design variables, and biology tunes them independently.

The architecture is the real insight. The engineered systems that best achieve this — flow batteries, at 10,000 to 27,000 cycles — got there by separating the carrier from the transducer. The energy-bearing fluid circulates; the expensive machinery stays fixed and is serviced separately. That is precisely the ATP architecture, and it may matter more than reversibility itself.

SystemUsable cyclesWhat limits it
Supercapacitor~10⁵–10⁶No chemistry to degrade

Read the last row against the rest. That is where the neglect actually lives. Cycle life is a headline specification in batteries — it is not ignored there. It is ignored in thermochemical and chemical storage, where energy-density chauvinism genuinely dominates. A celebrated salt-hydrate result reads "full cyclability for at least ten cycles," while organic flow chemistries are quoting fade below 0.001% per cycle.

The hypothesis is already published — by domain experts, about their own field. A 2026 review of salt-hydrate thermochemical storage states it directly: "marginal gains in theoretical energy density are far less impactful than improvements in cyclic stability, transport properties, and mechanical integrity."

Worth stealing from the flow-battery literature: they report fade per cycle and per day as separate numbers, because calendar-time chemical decomposition often dominates cycling damage. Any measurement here should keep those apart.

Soft triggers — how far the principle reaches

Rhenium-187 — a factor of a billion

Neutral, it has one of the smallest decay energies known, so decay is nearly phase-space forbidden. Strip all 75 electrons and the emitted electron can land in a bound orbital of the daughter instead of the continuum, raising the effective energy release about thirtyfold. Nothing was added to the nucleus. A different door opened.

neutral: 4.2 × 10¹⁰ yr
fully stripped: 32.9 yr
Q: 2.47 keV → ~73 keV

Dysprosium-163 — stable until you strip it

The inverse case, and the cleanest proof that the electron shell is a switch on nuclear stability rather than a rate modifier. As a neutral atom the decay is energetically forbidden outright. Remove the electrons and it becomes allowed.

neutral: stable
fully stripped: 47 days

Chemical environment — real but small

The honest ceiling for anything chemically accessible. Beryllium-7 sealed in a fullerene cage shifts by under one percent, and beryllium is the most favorable case in the periodic table. For heavier nuclei the inner shells dominate and chemistry moves the rate by 10⁻⁴ or less. Do not extrapolate from the storage-ring results — a bare relativistic nucleus is not a chemical environment.

Be-7 in C₆₀: 0.83%
vs gold host: ~1.2%
heavy nuclei: ≤ 10⁻⁴

Why tantalum and hafnium stay shut

Structural, not technological. Both have high transition multipolarity and high transition energy — exactly the regime where electron-shell coupling cannot reach. Thorium-229 succeeded in 2024 for the opposite reasons: only 8.4 eV up, low multipolarity, within reach of a laser through an electronic bridge. Separately, a 2018 claim of electron-capture triggering did not replicate — an independent isomer-beam experiment found nothing at 500× below the original limit.

A second unclaimed opening — and a $100 experiment

No unified framework treats usable cycles as the primary figure of merit across domains. Four literatures each hold one piece: biology has catalytic-cycles-until-replacement, industrial biocatalysis has total turnover number for the same quantity under a different name, batteries have cycle life and fade rate, and thermochemical storage has cyclic stability. The citation networks barely overlap.

The tabletop version is genuinely accessible. Salt hydrate cycling: calcium chloride or Epsom salt, a toaster oven, a 0.01 g kitchen scale, a thermocouple, a sealed jar. Dehydrate, weigh, rehydrate over water vapor, weigh, log the temperature rise. Plot mass swing and peak ΔT against cycle number for fifty-plus cycles, reporting fade per cycle and per day separately.

Unregulated materials, kitchen equipment, under $100 — and it measures the exact variable the field's own experts say is under-served. That is a rare combination.

Part IV — What a witness sees

Appearance, by mechanism

Because reported appearance is the most common input to this register, and it is more diagnostic than it looks.

MechanismVisible?What it looks like
Thermal gradient / plume Yes Shimmer and wobble; objects behind it swim and distort; emits no light of its own. The strongest candidate for a reported "heat-haze" distortion.
Air plasma (arc, corona, RF) Yes Self-luminous blue-violet or pink, a strong diverging distortion around it, and a co-located thermal plume. Gives itself away by ozone smell and RF or audio noise.
Femtosecond filament Yes, striking A thin straight free-floating glowing white-blue string, sharply localized, often with a coloured conical halo. Looks like nothing mechanical.
Flux pinning Not optically The object simply hangs, at any tilt, including inverted. No distortion of light. The tell is frost and vapour from the cryogen.
Diamagnetic levitation Not optically The object hangs — inside a magnet bore. No optical signature at all.
Acoustic levitation Barely Millimetre particles hanging in a row at fixed half-wavelength spacing; faint distortion at high sound pressure. Strong ultrasonic signature.

Read the table as a filter. The only mechanisms producing a visible, localized, apparently free-floating distortion in open air are a thermal gradient, an air plasma, or a laser filament — and the first two are far more common than the third. A report of "the air bent around it" is therefore consistent with something quite ordinary, and does not by itself indicate anything unusual.

Part V — Protocols

Eight measurements that separate candidates

Ordered by information yield per unit effort. Most need equipment you can hold in one hand.

P1

Material-dependence probe setHighest yield

Present PTFE, aluminium, bismuth and water to the same barrier and compare. Diamagnetic force scales as χ/ρ (bismuth ≈ 18× water); eddy currents act only on conductors; acoustic force follows acoustic contrast; gas-dynamic pressure is material-independent. One probe set, every candidate separated.

P2

Force–distance curve

Push a non-magnetic, non-conducting, low-susceptibility probe on a load cell and map F(d). A gradient force gives the 1/r⁷ signature. Radiation or acoustic pressure gives a flat force over one wavelength then a sign flip. A gas-dynamic barrier gives roughly constant pressure.

P3

Magnetometry at 0.1–3 m

A fluxgate or Hall survey settles this in seconds. Diamagnetic exclusion at any useful force requires B·∇B on the order of 10³ T²/m locally. Absence of a large field is fatal to the flux-pinning, diamagnetic and plasma-confinement candidates simultaneously.

P4

Optical distortion sign

Image a distant grid or point source through the region. Diverging versus converging, and the presence or absence of a buoyant plume, separates plasma from thermal immediately. This is a camera and a printed target.

P5

Emission spectroscopy

A transmission grating in front of a camera costs tens of dollars. Nitrogen second-positive bands plus the 777 nm oxygen line identify air plasma unambiguously; broadband supercontinuum identifies filamentation; a blackbody curve identifies incandescence.

P6

Thermal imaging and contact temperature

Flux pinning requires below 92 K for the common high-temperature superconductor. An IR camera and a thermocouple confirm or eliminate every cryogenic mechanism at once.

P7

Acoustic and ultrasonic survey

Acoustophoresis at any scale needs above ~155 dB somewhere and a standing wave with fixed half-wavelength node spacing. Measure the node spacing, derive the wavelength, derive the frequency. If it doesn't match, it isn't acoustic.

P8

Output-versus-angle curve, swept both directionsSpec 02

One-degree steps through a full revolution, clockwise then counter. Hysteresis separates bistable magnetic and mechanical switching from everything optical or diffractive. Then fit cosn(kφ): k = ½ is nested Halbach, k = 2 is a half-wave plate, k = 4–5 is a multipole flux switch. Count the nulls per revolution as a cross-check.

P9

Frequency sweep to find the cutoff edgeSpec 04

A signal generator and a receiver locate the transmission edge, and the edge is the electron density — read it off directly. Repeat with the source off: a plasma cutoff vanishes and moves with conditions, while a geometric aperture cutoff is fixed by the hardware and stays put.

Part VI — Using this

How rows get added

Enter a behavior only as an observable, with units where they exist. "Excludes matter at roughly one metre in open air" is a row. "Uses an unknown energy source" is not — it names an absence, and absences cannot be measured.

Then fill the two columns that matter. List every mechanism that produces that observable, with its real magnitude and its scaling law. Then name the measurement that separates them. A row without a third column is a note, not a register entry.

Three outcomes are possible when a row is complete, and all three are useful:

OutcomeWhat it meansWhat you do
Reduces to ordinary physics The observable is consistent with common mechanisms, so it carries no discriminating information. Stop spending attention on it. Note the reduction so nobody re-litigates it.
Multiple live candidates Several mechanisms fit and the report doesn't separate them. Run the discriminating protocol. This is where measurement actually pays.
No candidate at any magnitude Nothing known produces this observable, and you can state the scaling reason why. This is the genuine anomaly, now sharply enough stated to be worth someone's time.

The discipline that makes this work

Testimony sets the specification. It never sets the mechanism. That separation is what lets the register stay useful no matter how any particular account is eventually assessed — the rows are about physics, and physics does not depend on who reported what.

As of this revision the register holds five specs, and four have reduced. Light-bending, rotational switching, frequency-selective transmission and the nested geometry all have ordinary mechanisms with real numbers — so those observables carry no discriminating information, and two of them now yield measurements rather than puzzles.

One row remains in the third category: matter exclusion at standoff distance in open air. That is a much shorter list than we started with, which is the only honest form of progress here. It also means any future effort has a single clear target instead of five vague ones.
